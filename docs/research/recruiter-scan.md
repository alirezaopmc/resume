# Recruiter scan

Rule: the first screen answers who this is, what they do now, when, and where they studied. Bold the role. Keep that block in one column under a short header.

## What the studies measured

Ladders’ 2012 eye-tracking study reported that recruiters spent about six seconds on an initial fit decision, and about 80% of that time on six items: the name, the current title and company, the current dates, the previous title and company, the previous dates, and education. Detail below those items did not drive the first decision. Source: Ladders 2012 PDF, “Keeping an Eye on Recruiter Behavior”.

The 2018 study, released by Ladders on 6 November 2018, measured an average initial screen of 7.4 seconds. Resumes that held attention shared three traits: a simple layout with marked section and job titles, an F-pattern or E-pattern arrangement with bold job titles and short bullets, and an overview at the top of page 1. Resumes that lost attention were cramped, multi-column, thin on white space, weak on headers, and stuffed with keywords. An engaged reader spent similar time on page 2 as on page 1. Time on page 2 depended on page 1. Pages after page 2 drew little attention regardless. Source: Ladders 2018 PDF and the Ladders press release of the same study.

## How that maps onto a page

Nielsen Norman Group’s 2006 eyetracking study (232 users, web pages) described an F: a full horizontal sweep at the top, a shorter sweep below it, then a vertical scan down the left edge. The first lines carry more words than later lines. The first words of a line carry more fixations than later words. A later NN/g article states that the F is what happens on undifferentiated text, and that headings and spacing can shift the reader into a layer-cake pattern: stop on each heading, skip the section if it is irrelevant.

For this resume the layer-cake is the goal. Section labels and job titles are the headings. The left edge of each line starts with the role or the company, because that is the stem of the scan. A second column on the right is the region NN/g found people often skip, and it is the region Ladders associated with weak resumes.

## Scan band on this resume

A4 is 297 mm tall. Forty percent of that is about 119 mm. The header (name, title, one contact line) sits above the band and must stay short enough that the band still occupies the upper part of the page.

The band contains, in order:

1. A short summary paragraph. The degree is named here so education is visible in the first screen even though the Education heading comes later in the file.
2. Skills as two labelled groups (Primary, Familiar). A label scans faster than a prose sentence of tool names, and the groups use the `-` marker from ADR-002.
3. The latest role: bold title, company, dates, location, two or three bullets, and a Tools line.

The next recent roles continue on the rest of page 1, still under Experience.
